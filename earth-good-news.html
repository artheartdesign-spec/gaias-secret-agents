AUSTRALIA • Koala science

Australia is keeping a closer eye on koalas

Koalas can be tricky to count because they hide high in trees. On 18 November 2026, Australia's environment department updated its National Koala Monitoring Program information. It confirms a further $5 million for 2026–2028 to continue monitoring, bringing program investment to $15 million.

What has happened? Monitoring is established and additional funding is committed. This is not evidence that koala numbers have increased.

Agent question: How could scientists count an animal that's difficult to see?

Source (updated 18 Nov 2026): Australian Government — National Koala Monitoring Program.

AUSTRALIA • Wildlife protection

Two rare plants are being assessed

Australia's environment department updated its threatened-species consultation list on 12 November 2026. Two kinds of South Australian correa plants are open for public comment about possible threatened-species listing and conservation actions until 22 December.

What has happened? The assessment consultation is open; protection has not yet been decided. Even small plants deserve careful attention.

Agent question: Why might a tiny plant matter to insects and other wildlife?

Source (updated 12 Nov 2026): Australian Government — listing assessments.

WORLD • Nature and climate

Albania joins a team helping nature

On 17 November 2026, the International Union for Conservation of Nature announced that Albania joined the ENACT Partnership, which supports nature-based solutions such as protecting and restoring ecosystems.

What has happened? Albania joined the partnership. Joining does not mean restoration projects are already complete.

Agent question: How could healthy forests help people and animals at the same time?

Source (17 Nov 2026): IUCN — Albania joins ENACT.

WORLD • Progress check

Nature plans need real action

On 17 November 2026, IUCN reported findings from a new review of national nature-based-solution policies. The report examined documents from 127 countries and found encouraging links between nature, climate and land policies, but important gaps in funding, fairness and implementation.

What has happened? A new evidence review has been released. It is a guide to where more action is needed, not proof that all plans have succeeded.

Agent question: What's the difference between promising to plant trees and checking that they survive?

Source (17 Nov 2026): IUCN — ENACT global report.

This week’s secret mission • 15–20 minutes

Mission: The Leaf and Shade Detectives

Your assignment: Spend 15 minutes finding how plants offer shelter and shade to wildlife.

  1. Find a safe patch of garden, lawn, park or schoolyard. Leave animals, nests and plants undisturbed.
  2. Look carefully for three kinds of homes: under leaves, on bark, amongst flowers, or in the soil.
  3. Sketch a mini-map. Label the places offering food, water or shelter.
  4. Choose one small helpful action with an adult: leave some leaf litter in a suitable garden corner, plant a locally appropriate native plant another day, or simply protect the habitat by not disturbing it.

Agent question: “How can a place that looks messy to us be useful to wildlife?”

Safety: supervise children near water, roads and unfamiliar plants; do not touch wildlife, fungi or animal droppings. Wash hands afterward.

Family talk: Would you rather…?

Help scientists count koalas or help protect a rare flowering plant? Explain your choice, then swap sides and argue for the other animal.

There is no wrong choice. The goal is to listen, imagine and learn together.

Teacher corner: 10-minute classroom starter

Suitable for: Australian primary classrooms (adaptable, especially Years 2–6). Preparation: none; read one sourced story aloud and display or print the prompts.

  • 2 minutes — Notice: Which facts tell us something actually happened?
  • 3 minutes — Think: What does this species need from its habitat?
  • 3 minutes — Discuss: What is the difference between joining a partnership and measuring conservation results?
  • 2 minutes — Act: Each student draws one practical way to care for local habitat.

Skills: evidence literacy, habitats and biodiversity, speaking and listening, and informed action. Teachers can align this activity with their year-level curriculum; this is not a formal curriculum mapping.
