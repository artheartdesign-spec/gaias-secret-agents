## 1. Mission objective

**Mission objective:**  
Trace where water can travel around a familiar place, map the pathway safely, and choose one practical action that helps keep runoff cleaner.

Child-facing version:

> **OBJECTIVE:** Follow the water. Map the route. Protect what travels downstream.

The mission should teach connections without implying that every local drain or gutter leads to the same destination. Stormwater systems vary, so the child records what can be observed and marks unknown sections as **UNCONFIRMED**.

Mission 02 ended when Sprig found:

## 3. Story structure — three A4 landscape pages

### Story Page 1 — The Broken Blue Line

Astra receives Sprig’s partial map.

A blue line begins beside the Pollinator Pause Zone, crosses a path, reaches a drain symbol—and stops.

The rest of the route has been torn away.

Astra notices three things:

- a roof symbol,
- a street-edge symbol,
- a small waterway mark,
- and one handwritten word: **GONE**.

A second note has been added:

> ONCE IT LEAVES HERE, IT DOESN’T MATTER.

Astra does not accept the claim.

She sends the Agent a mapping file.

**Continuity reward:** children who completed Mission 02 recognise the exact water-drop symbol from Sprig’s final evidence.

End beat: Astra discovers several different blue lines on an older Headquarters map. They join farther away.

---

### Story Page 2 — Nothing Stays in One Place

Astra explains the mission in simple terms:

Rain can land on roofs, plants, soil, concrete and roads.

Some water soaks into the ground.

Some is collected.

Some travels downhill.

Some enters drains or channels.

Where it goes after that depends on the local system.

The Agent’s task is not to guess the final destination.

It is to map the **visible part of the journey** and mark the rest:

> **UNCONFIRMED — FURTHER INVESTIGATION REQUIRED**

Astra notices Dr. Null has circled one ordinary object beside the broken line: a piece of litter.

End beat: the Agent is asked to inspect a safe local route and look for things that water could carry with it.

---

### Story Page 3 — The Route Reappears

The Agent completes the dry-day Water Path Map.

Astra overlays the new evidence with Headquarters records.

The broken line no longer looks like a dead end.

It is part of a connected system.

The Agent has shown that a small action in one place can affect another place farther away.

Astra awards **Water Investigator** status and records the Agent Super Power:

**CONNECTIONS**

Then Headquarters receives one final fragment from the damaged map.

It contains no title.

Only three marks:

- a habitat symbol,
- a missing label,
- Dr. Null’s handwriting:

> ONE PLACE CHANGES. NOTHING ELSE DOES.

Astra turns the fragment over.

> “That,” she says, “is exactly what we need to test next.”

**Mission 04 remains CLASSIFIED.**

## 5. Field Guide — Where Water Can Go

One focus only: **water pathways**.

Child-facing pathway examples:

- roof → gutter
- garden → soil
- path → low point
- driveway → street edge
- street edge → drain
- drain → **destination may be unknown**

Key message:

> Agents record what they can see. Unknown is better than a made-up answer.

Explain:

- water often moves downhill,
- hard surfaces can create more surface flow than absorbent ground,
- soil and plants can slow or absorb some water,
- stormwater drains are not rubbish bins,
- local drainage systems differ.

### Safe observation rules

- do the field investigation in dry, calm weather,
- stay off roads,
- do not enter drains, channels, creeks or floodwater,
- do not lift grates or covers,
- observe from a safe public or household area with an adult,
- stop if conditions change.

## 6. Hands-on activity — Water Path Map

### Mission objective repeated

**Follow the water. Map the route. Protect what travels downstream.**

### Step 1 — Choose a starting point

Pick one safe place where rainwater could land:

- roof edge
- garden
- balcony
- paved path
- driveway edge
- park path

### Step 2 — Predict

Draw an arrow showing where you think water would move.

Mark it:

**PREDICTION**

### Step 3 — Look for evidence

Without using water, look for:

- slope
- gutter direction
- drain location
- low spots
- stains or debris lines
- soil versus hard surface
- plants that might slow flow

### Step 4 — Map only the visible route

Draw arrows along the part you can safely observe.

If the route disappears into a pipe, drain or area you cannot access, write:

> **UNCONFIRMED**

### Step 5 — What could travel with the water?

Tick only what is actually present nearby:

- leaves
- soil
- litter
- pet waste
- nothing obvious
- other

Do not touch unsafe material.

### Step 6 — Compare

Ask:

- Did my prediction match the evidence?
- Where did the route become unknown?
- What could I investigate another day?

## 7. Real-world action — Keep It Out of the Water Path

With a grown-up, choose **one** action that fits the place.

Options:

- put loose litter in the bin before rain can move it,
- sweep outdoor debris into an appropriate bin or compost rather than hosing it toward a drain,
- make sure paint, oil or household chemicals are never poured into a stormwater drain,
- keep pet waste away from places where rain can wash it into runoff,
- protect a small patch of soil or plants that helps slow water.

Child-facing line:

> **Small place. Small action. Connected system.**

The child should not handle hazardous waste, chemicals, sharp litter or drain infrastructure.

## 12. Twelve Secret Meeting Cards

These remain a family-and-friends question game.

Standard structure: **4 TALK + 4 IMAGINE + 4 DO**.

### TALK

1. **Where is your favourite place to be when it rains, and what do you like about it?**
2. **What is one place near you that looks completely different after rain?**
3. **If you could protect one river, creek, beach, pond or wet place anywhere, which would you choose?**
4. **What everyday thing do you think travels farther than people realise?**

### IMAGINE

5. **Imagine one raindrop could tell you its whole journey. Where did it begin and where did it end?**
6. **Design a secret Agent vehicle that can follow water anywhere without disturbing nature.**
7. **Imagine a hidden city built under a rain cloud. What happens there when the first drop falls?**
8. **A map has one missing blue line. Invent the strangest possible place it could lead.**

### DO

9. **Use your finger to draw an invisible water route in the air. Everyone else guesses where it is going.**
10. **Take turns making the sound of water in a different place: roof, puddle, creek, tap, storm or wave.**
11. **In 15 seconds, make a group pose called “The Great Raindrop Investigation.”**
12. **Choose three nearby objects and arrange them as a pretend landscape. Take turns describing where imaginary rain would travel.**

Anyone may pass. There are no wrong answers.

The cards do not count toward mission completion or rank progression.

## 13. Parent Guide

### Purpose

This mission introduces systems thinking through a concrete, visible idea: water moves between places.

### Important accuracy note

Do not tell children that every stormwater drain leads directly to a creek, river or ocean. Local systems vary.

The mission deliberately uses:

> **UNCONFIRMED**

when the destination cannot be safely observed.

### Adult role

Help the child:

- choose a safe observation area,
- stay away from roads and drains,
- notice slopes and surfaces,
- distinguish evidence from assumptions,
- choose a realistic household action.

### Useful questions

- What can you actually see?
- Where does the route become unknown?
- What might move with the water?
- What would you need to learn to confirm the next part?
