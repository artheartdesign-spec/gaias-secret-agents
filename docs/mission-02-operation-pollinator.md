# Mission 02 — Operation: Pollinator

**Status:** Design-ready mission architecture  
**Audience:** Ages 6–11  
**Format:** Full Gaia’s Secret Agents mission  
**Primary environmental theme:** Pollinators / biodiversity  
**Agency skill:** Observation + classification  
**Agent Super Power:** Observation  
**Earned badge:** Pollinator Specialist  
**Character lead:** Sprig — Tree Sprite, Forest Scout  
**Character File:** 004 — SPRIG  
**Bridge forward:** Mission 03 — The Vanishing Water

> Product rule: Mission 02 is an episode of the same Agent story, not a separate activity pack. It must reward children who completed the Starter Kit and Mission 01, but still make sense to a child entering at this point.

---

## 1. Mission objective

**Mission objective:**  
Investigate which visitors are using local flowers, record what is actually observed, and protect one small place where pollinators can feed safely.

Child-facing version:

> **OBJECTIVE:** Find the flower visitors. Record the evidence. Protect one pollinator stop.

The mission must avoid asking the child to prove why pollinators are present or absent. One short observation can reveal patterns, not establish a scientific cause.

---

## 2. Opening hook

Secret Headquarters receives an incomplete field report from Sprig.

At one of the Agency’s garden watch points, the flower records do not match.

One page says:

> NO VISITORS. NOTHING TO REPORT.

Another page contains tiny pollen marks, a wing-print sketch, and four missing visitor symbols.

Sprig believes someone has filed the evidence too quickly.

A second mark has been added in a different hand:

> IF YOU DON’T SEE IT, IT ISN’T THERE.

The wording is typical Dr. Null interference: dismissive, simple and easy for a child to recognise without needing complicated villain lore.

Sprig asks the Agent to reopen the case.

---

## 3. Story structure — three A4 landscape pages

### Story Page 1 — The Empty Visitor Log

Sprig is checking a hidden Agency flower station after several days away.

The flowers are open, but the official visitor log has been stamped **NOTHING TO REPORT**.

Sprig notices that the page itself disagrees with the stamp:

- a dusting of yellow pollen,
- a bent petal,
- a tiny set of marks beside the flower sketch,
- four blank spaces where visitor symbols should be.

Sprig does not assume what happened.

He sends the file to the Agent with one instruction:

> “Don’t guess. Look.”

**Continuity detail:** the recovered page carries a small **FOUND** mark from the Secret Headquarters archive. Children who completed the Starter Kit will recognise the idea of evidence being found and re-filed.

End beat: Sprig discovers that one corner of the visitor log has been deliberately folded over.

---

### Story Page 2 — The Wrong Label

Under the fold is a second label:

> NO VISITORS = NO POLLINATORS

Sprig immediately rejects the conclusion.

A flower can be unvisited during one observation and busy later. Wind, time of day, weather, flower type and simple chance can all change what an Agent sees.

Headquarters does not want a guess.

It wants a **field comparison**.

Sprig opens the Pollinator Watch protocol and assigns the Agent to observe real flowers nearby.

Dr. Null’s interference appears only as the wrong label and dismissive note. No spiral symbol.

End beat: one of Sprig’s older records shows a strange extra mark beside the busiest flower patch — a tiny water-drop symbol.

---

### Story Page 3 — The Visitors Return

The Agent completes the field watch and classifies what was observed.

Sprig compares the new record with Headquarters data.

The mystery is not solved by declaring that pollinators have “disappeared.” Instead, the Agent has done something more useful:

- recorded evidence,
- separated **seen** from **not seen**,
- classified visitors without needing to know every species,
- protected one small flower stop.

Sprig awards **Pollinator Specialist** status.

Then he notices the water-drop symbol again.

On the back of the old field record is a partial map. A blue line begins near a garden bed, passes a path and then stops beside a drain.

Someone has written:

> IT DISAPPEARS. SO WHAT?

Sprig sends the map to Secret Headquarters.

**Final line / Mission 03 bridge:**

> “Agent,” Sprig said, “I think our next question is where the water went.”

## 5. Field Guide — one focus only

# Flower Visitors

The guide should teach broad visitor groups rather than species identification.

Child-facing categories:

- **Bee**
- **Butterfly or moth**
- **Fly**
- **Beetle**
- **Bird**
- **Other / unsure**

Key message:

> You do not need to know its name to record good evidence.

Explain simply that pollinators move pollen between flowers while feeding or visiting. Not every visitor will pollinate every flower, and an Agent should record only what they actually observe.

### Look for

- landing on a flower
- moving between flowers
- spending time around the flower centre
- pollen visible on the body, if close enough to see safely

### Do not

- touch or catch insects
- disturb nests
- taste unknown plants
- enter private property
- approach stinging insects closely

Observe from a comfortable distance.

## 6. Hands-on activity — Pollinator Watch

### Mission objective repeated

**Find the flower visitors. Record the evidence. Protect one pollinator stop.**

### Step 1 — Choose the watch point

Find one safe flowering plant or small flower patch.

If there are no flowers nearby, use the **No Flowers Protocol** below.

### Step 2 — Watch for 10 minutes

Stay reasonably still.

Every time a visitor lands on or closely investigates a flower, make one mark in the correct visitor group.

Do not chase insects.

### Step 3 — Record conditions

Tick what applies:

- sunny
- cloudy
- windy
- still
- morning
- afternoon

### Step 4 — Record the evidence

Visitor count:

| Visitor | Marks |
|---|---|
| Bee | |
| Butterfly / moth | |
| Fly | |
| Beetle | |
| Bird | |
| Other / unsure | |

### Step 5 — What did you notice?

Tick any that are true:

- I saw more than one kind of visitor.
- Some flowers were visited more than others.
- I saw a visitor move between flowers.
- I noticed something I had not noticed before.
- I did not see any visitors during my watch.

Important child-facing note:

> **Zero is still evidence.** If you saw no visitors, record zero. Good Agents do not invent results.

### No Flowers Protocol

If there are no flowers available:

1. Search safely for three places where flowers **could** appear — a pot, garden bed, tree, shrub, grass edge or park planting.
2. Record whether each place has buds, leaves, old flower heads or no flowers.
3. Choose the best place to check again another day.

This keeps the mission usable without requiring flowers to be present.

## 7. Real-world action — Pollinator Pause Zone

Choose one existing flowering plant or flower patch.

For the next seven days, make it a **Pollinator Pause Zone**.

With a grown-up, choose at least one:

- leave some flowers unpicked,
- avoid spraying the flowers,
- keep the patch undisturbed where practical,
- check it again on another day,
- when your household next plants something, consider including a flowering plant.

Create a small paper sign:

> **POLLINATOR PAUSE ZONE — AGENT WATCH ACTIVE**

No purchase is required.

### Why this matters

Pollinators need safe places to find food. Leaving some flowers available and avoiding unnecessary disturbance gives visiting animals a better chance to use them.

## 12. Twelve Secret Meeting Cards

These remain a **family-and-friends question game**, not extra mission tasks.

Standard structure: **4 TALK + 4 IMAGINE + 4 DO**.

They may echo the pollinator/garden world, but they should stay social, funny and low-pressure.

### TALK

1. **If you could protect one tiny place in nature forever, what would you choose and why?**
2. **What is the smallest animal you remember noticing recently? What was it doing?**
3. **Would you rather have a garden full of colour, amazing smells or strange shapes? Why?**
4. **What is something in nature that other people walk past but you usually notice?**

### IMAGINE

5. **Invent a pollinator nobody has ever discovered. What does it look like and which flower does it love?**
6. **Imagine flowers could send secret messages. What would a flower outside your home say today?**
7. **Design a tiny airport for bees, butterflies and other flower visitors. What would be inside?**
8. **You discover a hidden garden used by Secret Agents. What is the first clue that tells you it is not an ordinary garden?**

### DO

9. **Take turns acting like a flower visitor landing on the world’s strangest imaginary flower.**
10. **One person becomes a flower. Everyone else chooses a different pollinator and shows how they would approach it.**
11. **Without speaking, act out something tiny you might notice outdoors. Everyone else guesses.**
12. **In 15 seconds, make the most dramatic “Agent discovers a microscopic clue” pose you can.**

### Secret Meeting protocol

Shuffle the cards.  
One Agent draws.  
Everyone answers or joins in.  
Anyone may pass.  
There are no wrong answers.

The cards extend the social life of the mission; completing all 12 is not required for mission completion.

## 13. Parent Guide

### Purpose

This mission develops careful observation and simple classification while introducing pollinators without requiring expert identification.

### What adults should know

- The child does not need to identify species.
- A short observation period cannot prove why visitors are present or absent.
- “No visitors seen” is a valid result.
- Adult supervision is required outdoors.
- Avoid approaching nests or handling insects.
- Do not use pesticides or garden chemicals as part of the activity.
- The action should remain small, achievable and positive.

### Useful questions

- What did you actually see?
- What are you unsure about?
- What would you check if you repeated the watch tomorrow?
- Did one flower seem busier than another?

Avoid supplying the “right answer” too quickly.
