# Gaia’s Secret Agents — Evidence-Informed Mission Standard

**Status:** Implementation standard  
**Audience:** Ages 6–11  
**Purpose:** Apply recent evidence on environmental education, hands-on learning and parent-child engagement without redesigning approved GSA work.

## Locked design principle

GSA missions should turn environmental learning into a sequence of:

**Discover → Do → Report → Influence**

The child remains the Agent and primary actor. The grown-up is a partner, not the teacher.

## Five evidence-aligned requirements

### 1. Real-world investigation

Every full mission should contain one safe activity that requires the child to observe, investigate, compare, make, test or map something in the real world.

The task should produce observable evidence rather than rely only on recall or worksheets.

### 2. Hands-on experiment where it genuinely fits

When the environmental topic supports it, include one **Agent Experiment** using inexpensive, ordinary materials.

Preferred pattern:

**Predict → Build/Do → Observe → Compare → Report**

The experiment must be optional where materials, weather or access make it impractical. It must never be required for mission completion when a safe observation alternative is available.

Do not add an experiment merely to satisfy the framework. The real-world investigation remains the priority.

### 3. Child agency

Missions should allow the child to make at least one meaningful choice:

- where to investigate,
- what to compare,
- what evidence to record,
- which small action to take, or
- what question to carry forward.

Open-ended exploration can be used deliberately. Not every outdoor activity needs to be a tightly scripted scavenger hunt.

### 4. Parent-child bridge

Every mission should contain one short **Grown-Up Briefing** or equivalent family conversation.

Use a specific evidence-based question rather than a generic discussion prompt. Examples:

- What did you actually see?
- What surprised you?
- What are you unsure about?
- What would you test next?
- What small change could our household make?

The child should be able to explain the discovery to the grown-up in their own words.

### 5. Influence action

The mission should finish with one small, realistic action that can influence the household or immediate environment.

Keep it:

- free or very low cost,
- achievable within days,
- environmentally positive,
- safe for a child,
- specific enough to observe whether it happened.

## What this means for existing missions

**Do not retrofit approved missions simply to add features.** Mission 02 — Operation: Pollinator and Mission 03 — The Vanishing Water already contain strong hands-on investigation, real-world action and Parent Guide/family-engagement elements. Their existing architecture should be preserved.

The main new requirement is therefore a reusable **Agent Experiment** module for future missions where the topic genuinely benefits from making/testing.

## First implementation target: next new mission

The next newly designed full mission should include one compact Agent Experiment if scientifically and practically appropriate.

A suitable pattern for a water-focused activity is a simple filtration investigation using safe household materials, where children:

1. predict which setup will allow water to pass most easily,
2. construct two or more simple models,
3. observe the differences,
4. record what happened,
5. report what the test can and cannot show.

The activity must explicitly avoid implying that a homemade filter makes water safe to drink. It is an observation/model experiment, not a water-treatment instruction.

## Family influence test

For each new mission, include one **Agent-to-Grown-Up Briefing** immediately after the field activity.

Suggested structure:

> **HEADQUARTERS BRIEFING**  
> Tell a grown-up what you found.  
> Show them your evidence.  
> Ask: “What is one small thing we could change this week?”

This preserves the existing GSA family-engagement model while making the child-to-household influence explicit.

## Evidence-to-product rule

Use the research to strengthen mission design, not to turn GSA into a school worksheet product.

Keep:

- story and character continuity,
- outdoor discovery,
- Agency artefacts,
- creative response,
- Secret Meeting Cards,
- badges and progression,
- parent guidance,
- safe alternatives for weather/access constraints.

Do not add:

- unnecessary quizzes,
- academic terminology,
- compulsory experiments for every mission,
- claims that GSA itself has been scientifically validated,
- claims that one activity proves a behavioural or environmental outcome.

## Success criteria for future missions

A new mission is evidence-aligned when a child can:

- investigate something real,
- do something rather than only read about it,
- distinguish observation from assumption,
- explain one discovery to a grown-up,
- choose or complete one small positive action,
- report what happened without needing to produce a “correct” result.
